## Summary
A practitioner overview of add-on strategy. It lists five common failure points, a KPI set including time-to-integration, and pre-close diligence items. It argues that centralized ERP/CRM and clean financial reporting are what exit buyers look for. It is light on master data specifics.

## Extracted claims
- [metric] Add-ons were nearly 74% of North American PE deal activity (citing PitchBook 2025 Global PE Report).
- [gate] Define the buy-and-build plan at platform launch: adjacency targets, integration model, funding limits.
- [gate] Build the 100-day plan before close, tied to the deal's value drivers.
- [failure-mode] No 100-day plan and no integration leadership, so value capture slows.
- [failure-mode] Overstated synergies with no accountable owner.
- [failure-mode] Founder-led teams resist new governance without change management.
- [failure-mode] Poor customer handoffs cause churn, service disruption and brand damage (customer master and continuity risk).
- [failure-mode] Underinvestment in systems: disparate platforms limit operational visibility.
- [timing] Top firms integrate key systems within six months of close.
- [metric] Time-to-integration (TTI) as a core KPI.
- [metric] Other KPIs: net revenue retention, SG&A as % of revenue, churn, employee retention, multiple arbitrage realization.
- [field] Centralized ERP and CRM support margin expansion; unified ERP, CRM and analytics give real-time control.
- [gate] Exit readiness: buyers look for unified systems and clean financial reporting.
- [gate] Pre-close diligence covers IT, software, cybersecurity and privacy, synergy validation, integration risk, supply chain and tariff exposure.
- [metric] Sellers with documented before/after metrics per add-on tend to get higher exit multiples.
- [field] Master data, CoA, cash-to-accrual and TSA not addressed (absence noted).
